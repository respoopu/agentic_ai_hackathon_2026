## F. Neuromyths (left/right brain)

🟢 Lim, S. W. H. & Bonga, M. "Beyond left and right: Learning is a whole-brain process." *Theory Into Practice*, Vol 61 No 3 (2022), pp. 347–357. DOI: 10.1080/00405841.2022.2096386
https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00405841.2022.2096386
→ "The argument of left- and right-brain learning is the second most pervasive neuromyth in education"; hemispheres show dominance for certain cognitive functions but "individual characteristics and learning potentials cannot be categorized into the left or the right brain."
**This is your strongest single citation. Use this one.**

🟢 Macdonald, K. et al. "Dispelling the Myth: Training in Education or Neuroscience Decreases but Does Not Eliminate Beliefs in Neuromyths." *Frontiers in Psychology* (2017). PMC5554523
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5554523/
→ Right-brain/left-brain learners factor together with learning styles, the Mozart effect, and the 10%-of-the-brain myth as "classic" neuromyths. General public endorsed 68% of neuromyths; educators 56%; high neuroscience exposure group 46%.

🟡 Boser, Ulrich. "Debunking The 'Learning Style' Neuromyth." *Forbes*, 4 Dec 2023.
https://www.forbes.com/sites/ulrichboser/2023/12/04/debunking-the-learning-style-neuromyth/
→ Attributes rejection of the left/right-brained framing to the American Psychological Association and Harvard. **Forbes is reporting this second-hand; if you want the APA position, cite APA directly.**

🟡 BrainPost summary, Sept 2023 — https://www.brainpost.co/weekly-brainpost/2023/9/12/myths-and-facts-left-brain-right-brain-function-learning-styles-and-sensory-processing
→ Points to **Nielsen et al. (2013)** as the key imaging study finding no evidence for hemispheric dominance in individuals. Worth chasing the original if you want a neuroscience citation rather than an education one.

🟡 Wharton Executive Education — https://executiveeducation.wharton.upenn.edu/thought-leadership/wharton-at-work/2023/05/insights-from-neuroscience/

## I. ⚠️ Claims in the Brief With NO Retrieved Source

**These came from my general knowledge or my own analysis. Do not cite them as researched findings. Verify or reframe before they go in front of judges.**

### Needs verification before use

**1. "Adolescent anthropometric talent ID is unreliable; maturation timing dominates; rankings often reverse by adulthood."**
This is a well-established finding in sports science, but I did not retrieve a source for it. Search terms: *relative age effect*, *biological maturation talent identification*, *bio-banding*, Malina/Cumming's work on maturity status in youth sport. Should be easy to source properly.

**2. "Body-composition feedback to adolescents carries body-image and disordered-eating risk."**
Also well-supported in the literature but not retrieved here. Search: adolescent body image, weight-related feedback, disordered eating risk factors in youth sport.
→ **Note:** even unsourced, this remains a sound design decision. You can justify cutting anthropometrics on caution and equity grounds alone without needing the citation.

**3. "Interest gets developed, not diagnosed."**
There is real theory behind this — Hidi & Renninger's four-phase model of interest development — but I asserted it without retrieving it. Worth grounding properly, because it's the intellectual justification for replacing the assessment with a trial loop.

**4. "CCAs are frequently assigned rather than freely chosen."**
My inference, not sourced. Plausible and widely experienced, but if a judge pushes back you have nothing. Either find data or soften to "capped by what one school offers" (which the Wikipedia/MOE source does support).

### My analysis, not external findings — label as such

**5. "Of ~40 winners reviewed, fewer than five are genuinely longitudinal."**
My own count across the projects listed in §H. It's a defensible observation from a real sample, but it is *our analysis*, not a published statistic. Present it as "we reviewed X hackathons and found…" — which is honest and actually more impressive.

**6. The five-archetype taxonomy** (Navigator / Sentinel / Researcher / Guardian / Coach).
Mine. Not an established framework. Fine to use — just don't imply it's an industry standard.

**7. The three-currency model** (money / time / tries) and the explore-exploit framing.
Our design contribution. This is the original thinking in the project — own it rather than sourcing it.

**8. "Class is the parent's mental model; scene is the teen's."**
My observation. No data behind it. Consider validating with even five informal teen interviews — that would convert it from assertion to primary research, and winning teams consistently do exactly this kind of small-n user research.
